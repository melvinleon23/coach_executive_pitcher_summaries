Executive Summary: CMJ & Fastball Velocity

Key findings, player differences, and organizational application

1. Player Comparison

Pitcher A

All four CMJ performance indicators demonstrated weak negative correlations with average fastball velocity (r = −0.163 to −0.216). No clear positive association emerged.

Pitcher B

Jump height, RSI-modified, and concentric mean power/BM demonstrated moderate positive correlations with average fastball velocity (r = +0.404 to +0.444). Eccentric mean power/BM showed a weak positive correlation (r = +0.102).

2. Key Findings & Take-Home Messages

Key finding: The relationship between CMJ performance and fastball velocity differed between the two pitchers.
Take-home message: CMJ performance should be interpreted at the individual-athlete level rather than assuming that better jump performance consistently corresponds to higher pitching velocity.
Practical implication: CMJ assessments can contribute to physical performance monitoring, but should complement pitching-specific measures rather than serve as standalone indicators of velocity.

3. Notable Differences Between Players

  • Direction of association: Pitcher A showed consistently weak negative relationships, whereas Pitcher B showed positive relationships across all four metrics.
  • Strength of association: Pitcher B demonstrated stronger relationships for jump height, RSI-modified, and concentric mean power/BM than Pitcher A.
  • Metric-specific behavior: Pitcher B's eccentric mean power/BM correlation was substantially weaker than the other three metrics, suggesting that different CMJ indicators may provide different information within the same athlete.

4. Application at a Larger Scale

These findings provide a starting point for an athlete-specific CMJ monitoring framework across a larger pitching population.

  • Establish individual baselines: Track each pitcher's typical CMJ performance and fastball velocity over time.
  • Evaluate within-athlete trends: Determine whether changes in specific CMJ indicators consistently accompany changes in fastball velocity for each pitcher.
  • Identify common patterns: With a larger sample, examine whether similar relationships emerge across pitchers or whether individualized interpretation remains most appropriate.

5. Future Data & Considerations

  • Pitching workload: Pitch counts, throwing volume, and recovery intervals may help explain game-to-game fluctuations in fastball velocity.
  • Readiness and recovery: Subjective readiness, soreness, and fatigue measures may provide context for changes in CMJ performance.
  • Additional physical qualities: Lower-body strength, rotational power, and mobility assessments could capture performance characteristics not directly represented by vertical CMJ testing.
  • Standardized testing schedules: Consistent timing between CMJ assessments and pitching appearances would improve longitudinal comparisons and reduce variability in assessment intervals.
